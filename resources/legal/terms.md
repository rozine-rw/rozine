## 2. How Rozine follows the rules for crowdfunding platforms

Platforms like Rozine must follow clear rules that protect investors and businesses. Here is each rule and how Rozine meets it.

1. **Investor money.** *The rule: the platform must not hold investor funds.* **How Rozine does it:** all customer money is to be held in a segregated client account at a licensed Rwandan commercial bank, kept apart from Rozine's own money. We are in talks with GT Bank to provide this account; no agreement has been signed yet. Rozine will not take real customer money until that account is open. Only Rozine's earned, disclosed fees ever move from the client account to Rozine.
2. **The platform's own interest.** *The rule: no raising funds for itself, and no holding interests in the businesses it lists.* **How Rozine does it:** Rozine never raises money on its own platform, never lends its own money and never holds a stake or a note in any business on Rozine.
3. **No guarantees.** *The rule: no guaranteed returns and no guaranteed success of a raise.* **How Rozine does it:** Rozine guarantees neither. A risk warning appears on every listing. If a raise does not reach 100% within 30 days, every investor gets all of their money back.
4. **No investment advice.** *The rule: the platform must not advise or recommend.* **How Rozine does it:** Rozine gives no advice. Each business carries a rating label, such as Strong, Stable or Distressed, produced by a published formula from facts verified by a Certified Public Accountant (CPA). A label is a description, not a recommendation.
5. **Terms of the offer.** *The rule: the business sets the terms; the platform may set admission requirements.* **How Rozine does it:** the business chooses how much to ask for, the term (3 to 6 months) and whether to accept the offer. The return comes from the verified risk and is never more than 15% for the whole term. A note is sized so the business's verified cash covers each instalment at least 1.5 times.
6. **Eligible businesses and checks.** *The rule: established small and medium businesses, with checks on the business and its directors.* **How Rozine does it:** Rozine lists Rwandan businesses with verified annual revenue of RWF 30,000,000 or more. Their directors and owners are identified and screened. The rating looks at how long the business has traded, its profit, how steady that profit is, and its size. A Rwandan CPA visits and verifies each business in person before it is listed.
7. **Disclosure.** *The rule: a full offer document before the offer opens.* **How Rozine does it:** every raise has a Rozine prospectus with CPA-verified financials. The business's official representative signs it, and it is then locked, dated and kept on record. Investors can read it before they invest.
8. **Withdrawal.** *The rule: a 48-hour cooling-off period.* **How Rozine does it:** investors can withdraw a commitment at any time, for a full refund, until the raise reaches 100%. That is longer than 48 hours for most raises.
9. **Investor limits.** *The rule: limits set by the platform.* **How Rozine does it:** no investor may hold more than 50% of any raise. We will add a limit for retail investors if the CMA requires one.
10. **Selling early.** *The rule: secondary trading is allowed under fair, published rules.* **How Rozine does it:** sellers set their own price, never above what the note still has to pay out. The seller pays a 2% fee and the buyer pays nothing. The rules are in section 4.9.
11. **Use of funds and records.** *The rule: reasonable steps to check how funds are used, and full records.* **How Rozine does it:** a CPA visits every business with a live note each month and seals a report for investors before each repayment. Every ledger entry is permanent: it can never be edited or deleted, and any correction is a new entry.
12. **Instruments.** *The rule: shares, debt and other approved instruments are allowed.* **How Rozine does it:** Rozine focuses on short-term debt, because it is the instrument Rwandan small businesses and first-time investors understand and use most.

## 4. For investors

4.1. **What you buy.** A note is a fixed-return loan to one business. The business owes each holder directly, in proportion to the units they hold. One unit is RWF 5,000 of principal. Units are recorded on Rozine's ledger.

4.2. **Risks you confirm.** After your identity is verified, and before your wallet opens, you confirm on one screen that you understand these five statements: I can lose all the money I invest. There is no guarantee or collateral. Returns are paid only if the business pays. I may not find a buyer if I want to sell early. Rozine operates in the CMA sandbox under limits. If this wording changes, you see the new wording once and confirm it again.

4.3. **Return and schedule.** Each note pays a fixed total return, set when the note is issued, in equal monthly instalments over 3, 4, 5 or 6 months. The full schedule is shown before you commit.

4.4. **Raises.** A raise is open for up to 30 days or until it is 100% funded. If it is not fully funded after 30 days, every commitment goes back to the investors' wallets in full, and nobody pays a fee.

4.5. **Committing.** Every investment is a whole number of RWF 5,000 units. You can put several notes in a cart and pay for them in one checkout. Before you pay, you see the amount, every fee in RWF, your net return, the full schedule and the risk warning. If any figure changes before you confirm, the payment is blocked and the screen refreshes. Paying confirms you have read the prospectus for each note in the cart.

4.6. **Withdrawing a commitment.** The money you commit is set aside in your wallet. Until the raise reaches 100%, you can withdraw your commitment at any time and get all of it back. Once the raise reaches 100%, your commitment is final and the money goes to the business.

4.7. **Payouts.** On each due date, the instalment is collected from the business. Your share is paid into your wallet, less the earnings fee in section 8.

4.8. **Late payment and default.** If a business pays late, a late charge of 5% of the unpaid amount is added on the due date, on day 7 and on day 30, up to 15% of the instalment. Every franc of late charges collected goes to the note's holders. Rozine takes none of it. If the arrears are not cleared by day 31, the note is in default and legal recovery starts. Everything recovered goes to holders in proportion to their units. Late charges and recoveries are paid only when they are actually collected. Rozine does not cover shortfalls.

4.9. **Selling early (resale).** When resale is open, you may list units of a note that is not late, in default or disputed. You choose how many units to sell and the price per unit, in whole francs, up to what the note still has to pay out per unit. A listing lasts 7 days. A buyer's purchase reserves the units for 5 minutes and uses money already in their wallet. The money and the units change hands in one step. The seller pays 2% of the sale price. There is no fee for listing, cancelling or letting a listing expire. Trading in a note pauses around each due date until that month's payouts are posted. A sale needs a buyer, so it is never guaranteed.

4.10. **Withdrawing from your wallet.** Each withdrawal is between RWF 1,000 and RWF 2,000,000 and goes only to your own verified account. Rozine charges no withdrawal fee. Your provider's charge is shown before you confirm. An unusual request may be held for up to 24 hours for review. Money committed to an open raise or reserved for a purchase cannot be withdrawn.

## 5. For businesses

5.1. **Who may apply.** The business must be registered in Rwanda, have verified annual revenue of RWF 30,000,000 or more, and upload 36 months of bank and Mobile Money statements (12 months for a repeat issuer).

5.2. **Official representative.** One official representative acts for the business for every approval, acceptance and dispute. The person who signs up for the business confirms that they can bind it. A second signatory is never required on Rozine.

5.3. **Your offer.** Rozine's engine works out the rating, the amount and the return from a published formula and the verified figures. No staff member can change the result or override a failed check. The amount is the lowest of what you asked for, what your verified cash can carry at a coverage of 1.5 times each instalment, 35% of your verified annual revenue and RWF 100,000,000, in multiples of RWF 5,000. You choose the term (3 to 6 months) and whether to accept.

5.4. **Prospectus.** Before your raise opens, your official representative signs the prospectus. It is then locked and shown to investors.

5.5. **Monthly checks.** While a note is outstanding, you share the month's figures about 10 days before each due date, and a CPA verifies them in person. The CPA's sealed report is published to investors before the due date. You can dispute a sealed report within 24 hours; after that it is final. Rozine may also send a CPA for an unannounced visit, and you must give them access.

5.6. **Disbursement.** When your raise reaches 100%, the money is paid to your verified account, less the 3% platform fee.

5.7. **Repayment.** You repay the principal plus the fixed return in equal monthly instalments. Each instalment is collected from your Rozine wallet on its due date, and we remind you to fund the wallet beforehand. If your wallet is short, what is there is collected and the rest becomes overdue; money that arrives later is collected straight away. You may repay the whole balance early, but the return owed does not reduce.

5.8. **Late payment and default.** Late charges are added as described in section 4.8. They all go to your investors. If you clear all arrears and late charges before day 31, the note returns to normal. If not, the note is in default on day 31 and legal recovery starts under Rwandan law.

5.9. **Repeat raises.** A new raise can open only after your previous note is fully repaid. It can be at most 1.5 times the principal of that note.
