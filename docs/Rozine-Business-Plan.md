## The problem 

#### 2.1 The business side: profitable, bankable, unbanked 

A Rwandan business can be genuinely profitable — steady MoMo receipts, real margins, a growing customer base — and still be structurally unable to borrow. The reason is not risk. The reason is collateral. 

Commercial lending in Rwanda is underwritten against pledged assets: land title, vehicles, machinery. A distributor with RWF 40 M in annual throughput but no title deed is, in the eyes of a bank's credit committee, uncreditworthy — regardless of how reliably cash arrives. The consequence is a class of businesses that are too big for microfinance, too asset-light for commercial credit, and therefore frozen at exactly the size where capital would compound fastest. 

The alternatives are worse. Informal lenders charge rates that destroy the very margin the loan was meant to grow. Supplier credit is short and relationship-dependent. Equity is premature and, for most owners, unwelcome — they do not want a partner, they want inventory. 

W HAT T HE B U SINESS AC TU ALLY NEEDS 

Not a bigger loan. A right-sized one — an amount its own cash flow can service, on a fixed repayment it can plan around, priced on performance rather than on what it owns. 

#### 2.2 The investor side: savings with nowhere to go 

The mirror image of the credit gap is a yield gap. A Rwandan with RWF 500,000 in savings has a narrow menu: a bank deposit paying single digits, treasury instruments with high minimums and long lock-ups, listed equities on a thin exchange, or informal lending with no recourse. Property is the default — but it is illiquid, lumpy and inaccessible below several million francs. 

What does not exist is the middle: a product where a person can put a modest sum to work, for a known term, at a known return, into something they can actually understand — a real business, in their own country, whose numbers have been checked by a professional. 

#### 2.3 The root cause: verification is expensive 

Both sides of the market fail for one shared reason. Verifying that a small business earns what it claims has historically required either a full audit (too costly relative to a RWF 20 M facility) or trust in self-reported figures (worthless as an investment basis). 

Every attempted solution to SME finance in the region has stumbled here. Lend on selfreported revenue and you underwrite fiction. Demand an audit and the economics collapse. Rozine's entire architecture exists to make verification cheap enough to be routine and rigorous enough to be investable.

## The solution 

Rozine turns a verified cash flow into a tradable instrument. The mechanism has four moving parts. 

#### 3.1 Machine verification, human attestation 

A business applying to Rozine uploads its bank and mobile-money statements — not a business plan, not projections, not a self-declared revenue figure. Our parsing engine reads the documents, reconstructs the cash-flow history, and computes a defensible picture of what actually moves through the business. 

Machine parsing alone, however, cannot confirm that the business is real, that the premises exist, or that inventory is on the shelf. So every listing and every monthly report is co-signed by an Audit Partner: an ICPAR-accredited CPA who visits the business in person, captures geo-tagged photographic evidence through the Rozine Auditor app, performs agreed-upon procedures under ISRS 4400, and applies a cryptographic verification seal. 

Machines make verification cheap. Licensed humans make it credible. Neither alone is sufficient; together they are the product. 

#### 3.2 Capacity-based sizing 

Rozine never asks a business how much it wants. We compute how much it can safely service from verified cash flow, and offer that. A business may raise less than its capacity; it may never raise more. This single rule is the platform's most important risk control, because it makes over-lending structurally impossible rather than merely discouraged. 

#### 3.3 Fixed total return, not APR 

A note carries a flat total return — a single percentage of principal, paid across the life of the note — with a floor of 10% and a ceiling of 15%, regardless of tenor. A RWF 100,000 investment at 12% returns RWF 112,000 in total, whether the term is three months or twelve. 

This is a deliberate departure from annualised pricing, and it is the correct one for this market for three reasons: 

- Comprehension. "You get RWF 112,000 back" is understood by every investor. "12% APR over 90 days" is understood by almost none. 

- Short-tenor viability. Under APR, a 3-month note pays a trivial absolute amount and nobody funds it — which would kill exactly the seasonal, harvest-cycle, inventory-turn borrowing that Rwandan SMEs most need. The flat floor makes every tenor worth funding. 

- Honest incentives. Because the total cost is fixed, a business that repays early pays no less — so it borrows for the term it actually needs rather than gaming the clock, and the investor's return is never eroded by prepayment. 

The trade-off is explicit and accepted: a 12-month note is better value per franc-year for the business, and a short note is proportionally expensive. We publish this plainly. Businesses choose their own tenor.

#### 3.4 Monthly proof, enforced 

Verification is not a one-time gate. Between the 1st and 7th of every month, each funded business must upload fresh statements and receive an on-site Audit Partner cosignature. The resulting report — parsed inflow and outflow, liquidity coverage, health status, business and auditor photographs, and the verification seal — is published to every investor holding that note. 

Missing the window has consequences, not reminders. Late reports trigger SLA escalation; unremedied breaches freeze the Audit Partner's yield share and flag the note across the platform. Investors therefore hold an instrument whose underlying performance is re-proven every thirty days.

## The underwriting engine 

Rozine's credit logic is deterministic, auditable and published. There is no black box: a business can see exactly why it received the capacity, rating and yield it did, and a regulator can reconstruct any decision from stored inputs. 

#### 5.1 Cash flow available for debt service 

From the parsed statement history we derive, per month, gross inflow and gross outflow. Net operating cash flow is their difference; CFADS is that figure adjusted for owner drawings, non-recurring items and existing debt obligations, then trimmed for volatility so that a single exceptional month cannot inflate capacity. 

##### CORE FORM U LAS 

```
NOCFm = Inflowm − Outflowm
CFADS = trimmed_mean(NOCF) − ExistingDebtService − OwnerDraw
DSCR  = CFADS ÷ ProposedMonthlyRepayment
Capacity = (CFADS × TargetDSCR−1 × Tenor) ÷ (1 + TotalReturn)
```

Figure 1 — Capacity is solved backwards from serviceable monthly repayment, never forwards from a requested amount. 

#### 5.2 Decision tiers 

|Tier|DSCRband|Treatment|
|---|---|---|
|Tier 1 — auto-<br>approve|≥ 1.25×|Cleared by the engine subject to Audit Partner<br>co-signature and KYB completion.|
|Tier 2 — audit<br>band|1.00× – 1.25×|Requires enhanced on-site procedures and<br>manual underwriter approval; capacity is<br>reduced.|
|Tier 3 — decline|< 1.00×|Rejected. The business receives its computed<br>figures and specific, actionable guidance on<br>what must change.|



Declines are not dead ends. A rejected business keeps its Rozine profile, continues to upload statements, and is automatically re-scored as its cash flow improves — converting today's rejection into tomorrow's pipeline. 

#### 5.3 Pricing 

Total return is a function of the rating and the tenor, bounded by the platform floor and ceiling. A stronger business borrows more cheaply; a longer tenor carries a higher total return because the investor's capital is committed for longer. Every note lands between 10% and 15% inclusive.

## The rating 

Rozine publishes exactly one measure of quality. Early designs carried both a 0–100 platform score and a separate risk scale; we deliberately collapsed them, because two competing numbers force every user to ask which one matters and erode trust in both. 

The unified rating is expressed on a 0.0–5.0 scale with one decimal, always paired with a plain-language band and a colour. The word leads, the number supports, the colour reinforces. 

|Band|Rating|Meaning to an investor|
|---|---|---|
|||Deep, stable, well-covered cash flow with a clean|
|Strong|4.0 – 5.0|verification history. Selective by design — most healthy<br>businesses will not qualify, and that is the point.|
|Stable|3.0 – 3.9|Sound and serviceable. The healthy default for an<br>ordinary, well-run business; not a warning.|
|Weak|2.0 – 2.9|Thin coverage or volatile receipts. Investable only with<br>the elevated return that accompanies it.|
|Distressed|Below 2.0|Impaired. Not listable; existing notes enter active<br>monitoring and recovery.|



Two design decisions deserve explanation. First, the scale is out of five rather than one hundred: a five-point scale with one decimal is instantly legible to a first-time investor, carries no false precision, and mirrors the rating vocabulary people already use everywhere else in daily life. Second, the "Strong" floor sits at 4.0 rather than at the midpoint — so that "Strong" means genuinely exceptional, and businesses have something real to strive toward instead of a label handed out for being merely above average.

## The Audit Partner network 

The Audit Partner network is Rozine's most distinctive asset and its hardest-won moat. It converts audit from a cost centre into a distributed, incentive-aligned workforce. 

#### 7.1 Lifecycle 

1. Signup and biometrics — identity capture and device binding. 

2. ICPAR licence verification — membership number and practising certificate checked against the register, with expiry tracking and manual override at the Admin verification desk. 

3. In-app engagement signing — the master services agreement and ISRS 4400 agreed-upon-procedures terms are executed inside the app. 

4. Spatial dispatch — jobs are routed to accredited partners within a 30 km radius of the business, subject to live capacity limits. 

5. On-site inspection — premises visit, procedures performed, geo-tagged photographs captured in-app (camera only; no gallery uploads). 

6. Verification seal — a cryptographic hash binding auditor identity, licence, report contents and timestamp. 

7. Yield routing — the partner earns 25% of the service fee collected on every note they steward, paid monthly on satisfactory SLA performance. 

#### 7.2 Why CPAs participate 

An ICPAR-accredited accountant in Rwanda typically serves a modest local client base with lumpy, seasonal fee income. Rozine offers recurring monthly work within their own district, dispatched automatically, performed in a structured app, and compensated as a share of a growing yield stream rather than a one-off fee. As the notes they steward mature and renew, their income compounds. 

The alignment is exact: the partner is paid from the fee stream that only continues if the business keeps performing and keeps reporting. A partner who signs carelessly loses the annuity. 

#### 7.3 Controls against capture 

- Rotation. No partner may steward the same business indefinitely; assignments rotate on a fixed cycle. 

- Concurrency caps. Each partner carries a maximum number of live engagements, enforced by the dispatcher. 

- Evidence integrity. Photographs must be captured in-app with live 

- geolocation; EXIF and coordinates are stored and cross-checked against the registered premises. 

- Flash Audits. Randomly triggered 24-hour re-verifications test both the business and the partner. 

- Yield freeze. Admin can suspend a partner's payout pending investigation of any discrepancy, dispute or SLA breach. 

- Full audit trail. Every admin action against a partner is logged with actor, reason and timestamp.

#### 8.1 Why this structure is the right one 

It is capital-light. Revenue scales with volume through the platform, not with a balance sheet we must fund and provision. Growth does not require ever-larger equity raises to support ever-larger loan books. 

It is aligned. The largest single line — the service fee — is collected only when a business actually repays. If borrowers stop performing, our revenue stops with them. We cannot profit from distress. 

It funds its own quality control. A quarter of the service fee pays the Audit Partner. Verification is not an overhead we are tempted to cut; it is a variable cost that scales precisely with the book it protects. 

It compounds through liquidity. The secondary fee grows with trading activity, which grows with book size and investor count — a revenue line that improves as the network matures, at almost no incremental cost.

## Market 

Rwanda is the right first market for a verification-led capital marketplace, for reasons that are structural rather than sentimental. 

- Digital payment penetration is high. Mobile money is the default transaction rail for small businesses, which means the raw material for statement-based underwriting already exists in machine-readable form — the single precondition our model depends on. 

- Formalisation is advanced. Business registration and digital identity infrastructure are unusually strong for the region, lowering KYB friction and fraud exposure. 

- The regulator is engaged. The Capital Market Authority operates a genuine sandbox pathway, and national policy actively favours capital-market deepening and SME financing. 

- The professional body exists. ICPAR provides an accredited, disciplinable pool of accountants — without which the Audit Partner model could not be built at all. 

- The market is legible. Rwanda is small enough to reach comprehensively and to prove a model definitively, which is exactly what a regulator and a future regional expansion both require. 

#### 9.1 Segments we serve first 

Our initial focus is on businesses with verifiable digital receipts and short working-capital cycles, where the case for revenue-based finance is strongest and the verification signal is cleanest: distribution and wholesale, agri-processing and seasonal trade, retail and pharmacy chains, logistics, light manufacturing, and established service firms with recurring contracts. 

#### 9.2 The investor base 

We build the retail side deliberately, in three concentric rings: salaried professionals in Kigali who already save digitally; the diaspora, who want a productive channel into the domestic economy beyond remittance and property; and institutions — SACCOs, cooperatives, funds and family offices — for whom Rozine notes offer a verified, shortduration, domestically denominated asset with no origination overhead. 

The RWF 5,000 minimum is a strategic instrument, not a marketing gesture. It converts saving from an act of accumulation into an act of participation, and it makes the platform's growth a function of how many Rwandans join rather than how few large cheques we can secure.

## Go-to-market 

Rozine's launch sequence is designed so that each stage de-risks the next, and so that we arrive at the regulator with evidence rather than intentions. 

#### Stage 1 — Institutional partnerships 

We do not acquire businesses one at a time. We partner with the organisations that already convene, train and finance them: enterprise-development networks, the privatesector federation and its ICT chamber, business-development advisory bodies, and existing SME investors and accelerators. 

Each partnership is structured around a concrete ask rather than a logo exchange: qualified referrals from their portfolio, a pilot cohort, co-designed eligibility criteria, or co-investment alongside the crowd. For the partner, Rozine solves a problem they already have — their graduates are investment-ready but uninvestable, and they lack a capital instrument to hand them. 

#### Stage 2 — Rozine Pulse 

Pulse is a public, pre-launch demand-aggregation campaign, and it is the strategic heart of the go-to-market. 

Investors register a non-binding pledge — an amount they intend to invest at launch. Businesses upload a single statement and receive an instant, real pre-qualification: the loan amount they would qualify for, their rating and their indicative yield. Both sides receive a designed, shareable pass that they distribute themselves, turning every participant into a distribution channel. 

Pulse delivers four things simultaneously, before a single franc is at risk: 

- Quantified demand on both sides — the exact evidence a regulator asks for. 

- A warm launch book — businesses pre-qualified and investors pre-committed on day one. 

- A live test of the parsing engine against real statements at volume. 

- Brand and virality at near-zero acquisition cost, through the shareable pass. 

Critically, Pulse collects no money and issues no instrument. It is a demand simulation, labelled as such, and therefore sits cleanly outside regulated activity. 

#### Stage 3 — RCMA sandbox entry 

With demand proven, the platform built and the Audit Partner cohort accredited, we enter the Capital Market Authority's regulatory sandbox: live operation at controlled scale, under defined caps and supervisory reporting. 

#### Stage 4 — Full licence and scale 

Sandbox performance data supports the application for a full operating licence, after which caps are lifted, the institutional investor channel opens fully, and the model is prepared for regional extension into markets with comparable mobile-money penetration and professional-accountancy infrastructure.

#### 14.1 Engineering principles 

- One source of truth. Every app reads and writes the same core. No app owns private state that others must guess at. 

- Deterministic and reconstructible. Any decision — capacity, rating, price,

- Money is a ledger, never a field. No balance is ever mutated; balances are derived from immutable entries.

## Vision 

The near-term goal is a functioning, licensed capital marketplace in Rwanda where a profitable business can raise growth capital in days on the strength of its own cash flow, and where an ordinary Rwandan can put RWF 5,000 to work in that business and be told the truth about it every month. 

The longer arc is larger. Every note Rozine originates, verifies, services and either repays or recovers becomes a permanent, structured record of small-business performance in an economy where no such record has ever existed at scale. Over years, that accumulated history becomes the credit infrastructure of the private sector itself — the dataset against which capacity can be assessed, risk can be priced, and capital can finally be allocated on evidence rather than on collateral. 

The Audit Partner network extends the same logic to the profession: a distributed, digitally equipped, continuously trained corps of accountants whose work is not an annual compliance exercise but a monthly act of market-making. 

And the model is portable. Any market with meaningful mobile-money penetration and a functioning professional-accountancy body has the two preconditions Rozine requires. Rwanda is where we prove it — deliberately, completely, and in full view of the regulator — because a model proven here can be trusted anywhere. 

##### W H AT W E A R E AC T U A L LY B U I L D I N G 

Not a lending app. A market — with its own instrument, its own price, its own liquidity, its own verification profession, and its own public record of who performed and who did not. Rwanda has a stock exchange for its largest companies. Rozine is the capital market for everyone else.
